# Onion Layout (v\<MERMAID_RELEASE_VERSION>+)

The **onion** layout draws a flowchart as an architecture diagram in the style of hexagonal (ports and adapters), clean and onion architecture: nested subgraphs become concentric hexagon rings around the domain, ports sit on the core's border, and adapters and the systems they talk to stand on straight spokes in line with their ports.

There are two arrangements of the same layout:

- `onion-vertical`: driving side at the top, driven side at the bottom.
- `onion`: driving side on the left, driven side on the right.

## How to use

Set the layout in the diagram's frontmatter config, nest the rings as subgraphs, and give each ring the hexagon shape with `@{ shape: hex }` (see Subgraph shapes on the [flowchart page](../syntax/flowchart.md)). This is the [bookmark service](https://github.com/VyacheslavLukin/hexagonal-service) example:

```mermaid
---
config:
  layout: onion-vertical
---
flowchart TD
    client[["HTTP client"]]
    subgraph adapters["ADAPTERS · src/adapters"]@{ shape: hex }
        subgraph driving["Driving side"]
            fastify["FastifyHttpServer<br/>REST routes /bookmarks"]
        end
        subgraph core["APPLICATION CORE"]@{ shape: hex }
            service_port[/"BookmarkServicePort<br/>«driving port»"/]
            subgraph app["BookmarkService"]@{ shape: hex }

                update["Update bookmark"]
```

## How the rings are read

Roles come from how the subgraphs nest, never from node ids or labels:

| In the diagram                                          | Drawn as                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| The outermost subgraph                                  | The adapters ring                                          |
| Its subgraph that holds further subgraphs               | The application core                                       |
| Subgraphs nested further in                             | Inner rings; the last one is packed at the centre          |
| Nodes directly in the core                              | Ports, on the core's border                                |
| A port whose edges mostly come in from outside the core | A driving port, on the driving side                        |
| A port whose edges mostly go out of the core            | A driven port, on the driven side                          |
| Nodes in the adapters ring, outside the core            | Adapters, on the spoke of the port they connect to         |
| Nodes outside every subgraph                            | External systems, beyond the adapters on that spoke        |
| Subgraphs in the adapters ring that hold only nodes     | Grouping only: their nodes are laid out, no frame is drawn |

The adapters ring is optional: without it, the core is the outermost ring and its side groups stand beside it.

A diagram needs at least three nested subgraphs: the adapters ring, the core and one ring inside the core. For any other shape the layout stops with an error that says what it found.

## Things to know

- Edges are straight lines. Labels are placed along their edge, clear of nodes, other labels, ring outlines and titles, and other edges where there is room.
- A use case that is called from a driving port and calls a driven port can cross its neighbour's edges; with straight lines that is not always avoidable.
- Ring colours come from your styles: the example shades the rings with `classDef`.
- The onion layout is not part of the tiny build.
